By Jason Powell , ProWrestling.net Editor ( @prowrestlingnet )

AEW Full Gear

Phoenix, Arizona, at Mortgage Matchup Center

Aired live on November 14, 2026, on pay-per-view

AEW Full Gear pre-show results: Jack Perry beat The Beast Mortos in 9:25, Kevin Knight, Wardlow, and El Clon beat Jordan Cruz, Jaden Monroe, and RJ Wylde in 2:05, Big Boom AJ, QT Marshall, Matt Sydal, and Darius Martin beat Rocky Romero, Trent Beretta, Josh Alexander, and Lance Archer in roughly 10:00, and Queen Aminata defeated Persephone, Hyan, and Skye Blue in a four-way in 10:55 to retain the TBS Championship…

The show opened with a Full Gear video package… Excalibur, Nigel McGuinness, and Tony Schiavone were on commentary. Excalibur said they would be on the call for the entire night…

A video package set up the AEW International Championship match… Adam Copeland made his entrnace while ring announcer Arkady Aura introduced him. Yes, the fans sang his entrance theme after it stopped playing. Kazuchika Okada made his entrance with Don Callis…

1. Kazuchika Okada (w/Don Callis) vs. Adam Copeland for the AEW International Championship. The fans cheered loudly before the opening bell. Okada and Copeland met in the middle of the ring and looked at each other, then at the cheering fans, who broke into an “AEW” chant followed by a “F— Don Callis” chant.

Okada went for an early lariat that Copeland avoided. Copeland charged for a spear, but Okada rolled out of the ring to avoid it. Okada ran Copeland into the ring steps. Okada pulled up a floor mat to expose the concrete. Okada set up Copeland for a move, but he reversed it by backdropping him onto the floor mat.

Copeland set up a chair and then atomic dropped Okada onto it before booting him in the face. Copeland maintained control until he decided to turn his attention to Callis. Okada took advantage of the distraction by dropkicking Copeland. Back in the ring, Okada put Copeland down with a neckbreaker.

Okada controlled the next few minutes of offense. Okada performed a top rope elbow drop. Okada sold elbow pain, and then flipped off the hard camera. Copeland came back briefly, but Okada put him down with a neckbreaker over his knee.

Okada went to the floor and returned to the ring with his title belt. The referee spotted it. Copeland rolled up Okada for a two count. Copeland stuffed a Rainmaker and put Okada down with a uranage slam. The wrestlers traded punches in the middle of the ring, and then Okada put Copeland down with a dropkick.

Copeland went for a spear, but Okada leapfrogged him, and Copeland hit the middle rope. Okada came right back and put Copeland down. Rather than go for a pin, Okada tried to show up Copeland by going for a spear, but Copeland caught him with an uppercut.

Copeland went to the middle of the ring and flipped off the camera. The fans popped. Copeland teased a spear, but Okada rolled under the bottom rope to the apron. Copeland wanted to spear him, but Callis grabbed his foot and distracted him. Okada grabbed his title belt from the floor and then waited for Copeland to charge with a spear, only to hold the belt in front of his head.

Okada performed a safe DDT off the apron onto the exposed concrete. Okada brought Copeland back inside the ring and covered him, only to lift him up when the referee made a one count. Okada did it again. Okada picked up Copeland, hit him with a Rainmaker, and pinned him…

Kazuchika Okada defeated Adam Copeland in 18:50 to retain the AEW International Championship.

Afterward, Copeland struggled to get to his feet. Once he did, Okada returned to the ring and put him right back down with a Rainmaker. Okada rolled to the floor and mockingly said, “I’m so sorry”…

Powell’s POV: It felt like the match had a TV finish meant to set up a pay-per-view rematch. But it was a good idea to have this in the opening slot. The fans were fired up for the legends, who didn’t have to follow the faster-paced matches. I thought Copeland might challenge for the AEW World Championship next month, but that finish has to lead to another match between the two.

A video package set up the AEW Tag Team Title match, and then the entrances followed…

2. “The Young Bucks” Matt Jackson and Nick Jackson vs. Mark Davis and Jake Doyle (w/Don Callis) for the AEW Tag Team Titles. Callis sat in on commentary. Yes, of course, Rick Knox was the referee.
