By Jason Powell , ProWrestling.net Editor ( @prowrestlingnet )

AEW Full Gear

Phoenix, Arizona, at Mortgage Matchup Center

Aired live on November 14, 2026, on pay-per-view

AEW Full Gear pre-show results: Jack Perry beat The Beast Mortos in 9:25, Kevin Knight, Wardlow, and El Clon beat Jordan Cruz, Jaden Monroe, and RJ Wylde in 2:05, Big Boom AJ, QT Marshall, Matt Sydal, and Darius Martin beat Rocky Romero, Trent Beretta, Josh Alexander, and Lance Archer in roughly 10:00, and Queen Aminata defeated Persephone, Hyan, and Skye Blue in a four-way in 10:55 to retain the TBS Championship…

The show opened with a Full Gear video package… Excalibur, Nigel McGuinness, and Tony Schiavone were on commentary. Excalibur said they would be on the call for the entire night…

A video package set up the AEW International Championship match… Adam Copeland made his entrnace while ring announcer Arkady Aura introduced him. Yes, the fans sang his entrance theme after it stopped playing. Kazuchika Okada made his entrance with Don Callis…

1. Kazuchika Okada (w/Don Callis) vs. Adam Copeland for the AEW International Championship. The fans cheered loudly before the opening bell. Okada and Copeland met in the middle of the ring and looked at each other, then at the cheering fans, who broke into an “AEW” chant followed by a “F— Don Callis” chant.

Okada went for an early lariat that Copeland avoided. Copeland charged for a spear, but Okada rolled out of the ring to avoid it. Okada ran Copeland into the ring steps. Okada pulled up a floor mat to expose the concrete. Okada set up Copeland for a move, but he reversed it by backdropping him onto the floor mat.

Copeland set up a chair and then atomic dropped Okada onto it before booting him in the face. Copeland maintained control until he decided to turn his attention to Callis. Okada took advantage of the distraction by dropkicking Copeland. Back in the ring, Okada put Copeland down with a neckbreaker.

Okada controlled the next few minutes of offense. Okada performed a top rope elbow drop. Okada sold elbow pain, and then flipped off the hard camera. Copeland came back briefly, but Okada put him down with a neckbreaker over his knee.

Okada went to the floor and returned to the ring with his title belt. The referee spotted it. Copeland rolled up Okada for a two count. Copeland stuffed a Rainmaker and put Okada down with a uranage slam. The wrestlers traded punches in the middle of the ring, and then Okada put Copeland down with a dropkick.

Copeland went for a spear, but Okada leapfrogged him, and Copeland hit the middle rope. Okada came right back and put Copeland down. Rather than go for a pin, Okada tried to show up Copeland by going for a spear, but Copeland caught him with an uppercut.

Copeland went to the middle of the ring and flipped off the camera. The fans popped. Copeland teased a spear, but Okada rolled under the bottom rope to the apron. Copeland wanted to spear him, but Callis grabbed his foot and distracted him. Okada grabbed his title belt from the floor and then waited for Copeland to charge with a spear, only to hold the belt in front of his head.

Okada performed a safe DDT off the apron onto the exposed concrete. Okada brought Copeland back inside the ring and covered him, only to lift him up when the referee made a one count. Okada did it again. Okada picked up Copeland, hit him with a Rainmaker, and pinned him…

Kazuchika Okada defeated Adam Copeland in 18:50 to retain the AEW International Championship.

Afterward, Copeland struggled to get to his feet. Once he did, Okada returned to the ring and put him right back down with a Rainmaker. Okada rolled to the floor and mockingly said, “I’m so sorry”…

Powell’s POV: It felt like the match had a TV finish meant to set up a pay-per-view rematch. But it was a good idea to have this in the opening slot. The fans were fired up for the legends, who didn’t have to follow the faster-paced matches. I thought Copeland might challenge for the AEW World Championship next month, but that finish has to lead to another match between the two.

A video package set up the AEW Tag Team Title match, and then the entrances followed…

2. “The Young Bucks” Matt Jackson and Nick Jackson vs. Mark Davis and Jake Doyle (w/Don Callis) for the AEW Tag Team Titles. Callis sat in on commentary. Yes, of course, Rick Knox was the referee.

The challengers were dominant to start, but the Bucks fought back, unlike when they had no offense during the eliminator match.

Davis and Doyle isolated Matt, who eventually put Doyle down with a DDT. Nick tagged in and ended up performing a crossbody block on Doyle, who tripped over Matt, which led to a two count. The Bucks set up for a move on the top rope in opposite corners, but Davis and Doyle cut them off and joined them on the ropes. The Bucks got the better of the exchange and performed simultaneous sunset bombs, followed by top rope elbows. Nick covered Doyle for a near fall.

Nick dove over the top rope toward his opponents, who caught him and put him down. Davis and Doyle returned to the ring and performed power moves on Matt. Doyle covered Matt, but Nick returned to break it up (it wasn’t very suspenseful because the camera caught Nick waiting on the apron).

Nick performed a facebuster on Doyle, then went to the apron and performed a tornado DDT on Davis on the floor. Back in the ring, the Bucks hit a BTE Trigger on Doyle. Matt covered Doyle, who kicked out aggressively while tossing Matt through the ropes to the floor.

The Bucks went for a double superkick, but Doyle caught their legs and slammed them to the mat. Davis returned to the ring and ran into a double superkick, but then he came back with power moves on both Bucks. Davis performed an awkward double piledriver and then got a near fall.

Doyle set up Matt for a move on the ropes, but Nick pulled him off with a German Suplex. The Bucks hit Davis with a Code Red and a BTE Trigger on Davis, who kicked out of Nick’s pin attempt. Doyle broke up a Bucks’ double-team move. Doyle powerbombed Nick onto the apron, and Nick landed on a table in the timekeeper’s area.

In the ring, Davis performed a piledriver on Matt and covered him for a near fall. An “AEW” chant broke out. Davis and Doyle went for simultaneous clotheslines on Matt, who avoided them, causing the challengers to clothesline one another. Davis grabbed Nick from behind and held him while his partner went for a clothesline, but Nick broke free, and Doyle clotheslined Davis over the top rope to the floor. Matt jumped off the middle rope at Doyle, who caught hima nd powered him up. Nick jumped from the ropes and pushed his brother, who performed a huracanrana on Doyle. Nick dove through the ropes and flew over Davis, but then he held him on the floor while Matt pinned Doyle…

“The Young Bucks” Matt Jackson and Nick Jackson defeated Mark Davis and Jake Doyle in 20:45 for the AEW Tag Team Titles.

After the match, the “Motor City Machine Guns” Alex Shelley and Chris Sabin made their entrance. Once in the ring, Shelley and Sabin pulled the Bucks to their feet and raised their hands.

A siren sounded and a logo listed “The Kasper Brothers” on the screen. Jacob Kasper and Drew Casper (f/k/a Julius Creed and Brutus Creed) headed to the ring and attacked the Bucks. The Guns tried to help, but the Kaspers got the better of them as well. Drew went to the ropes for a move, but he was distracted by entrance music.

“FTR” Dax Harwood and Cash Wheeler made their entrnace with Stokely Hathaway. Harwood and Wheeler entered the ring and went face-to-face with the Kaspers, but then both teams turned and attacked the Bucks and Guns. Jacob and Harwood picked up a title belt simultaneously, and Drew and Wheeler did the same.

Hathaway intervened and got both teams to let go of the title belts. Hathaway held up the title belts before dropping them in front of the Bucks. The Kaspers hugged Hathaway, who then shook hands with Harwood. Hathaway pulled in Harwood and Wheeler and hugged them. FTR left the ring. Hathaway followed. When the Kaspers left the ring, Drew looked into a camera and spoke about the best tag team wrestling on the planet and said “AEW” while spelling it with his index finger…

Powell’s POV: You had to know the Bucks would get their win back when they took that squash loss to Davis and Doyle in the eliminator match. I still give them credit for taking that lopsided loss, as it helped sell the title match, which was entertaining despite the outcome feeling predictable. The Kaspers showing up afterward was a fun surprise. I watch way too much wrestling and forgot about the Diamond Mine connection between Hathaway, who went by Malcom Bivens, and the Kaspers/Creeds in NXT during the brand’s 2.0 days. I’m sure I’m not the only one who forgot, but it’s nothing that can’t be explained in a brief promo. I guess the mystery now is which team Hathaway will end up with, as I doubt they’ll all be one big, happy family.

A video package set up the Texas Death Match. Swerve Strickland and Prince Nana were out first, followed by Kofi and Austin Creed.

A video aired from the Old West set that AEW used in the past. Brody King was in a saloon, and he ended up beating up several patrons while Hangman Page stood outside. A man charged when King ran out, so Page clotheslined him and then beat up two other men. Page took a shot. A guy hit Page from behind. Page tossed him into the street. Bandido showed up and stood over the man, who begged off while saying he thought Bandido was dead. The trio was shown riding off on horses.

Back in the building, Page, Bandido, and King made their entrance. Excalibur pointed out the new AEW Trios Title belts. He also said there’s a black arrow tribute to Pac (I believe he said it’s on a Full Gear logo)…

3. Hangman Page, Bandido, and Brody King vs. Swerve Strickland, Kofi, and Austin Creed in a Texas Death Match for the AEW Trios Titles. A table covered in barbed wire was on the floor in front of the broadcast team. Page, Bandido, and King put skewers in the heads of Swerve, Kofi, and Creed early in the match.

Powell’s POV: Call me Nostradamus. I told Jake Barnett during the Dot Net Weekly audio show on Friday that I had a vision of Austin Creed making over-the-top facial expressions with skewers stuck in his head. On a side note, WrestleTix listed the venue as being set up for 7,476, with 7,022 tickets distributed. The total number of seats on the map is 15,103. The last time AEW ran the venue, 5,248 tickets were distributed for the February 7, 2024, Dynamite.

Creed ended up under the ring. When he was pulled out, he had a trombone wrapped in barbed wire, which he used as a weapon before playing it. Page and King bled from their foreheads. Back in the ring, Page wrapped his arm in barbed wire from the table at ringside and hit all three opponents with it.

Later, Page and Swerve sat on chairs in the ring and used staple guns on one another. Kofi and Bandido tried to stop them, but they were both stapled before they rolled to the floor. Page put Swerve down with a lariat and went to the apron. Nana joined him on the apron. Page grabbed Nana and set up for a Deadeye, but Swerve low-blowed him. Bandido dropkicked Swerve, but then ran into a Kofi kick. Page returned and clotheslined Kofi. Creed superkicked Page. King put Creed down with a discus lariat and followed up with a piledriver on Kofi.

King pulled a glove covered in thumbtacks out from under the ring and put it on. King got the ine ring and chopped Swerve with it, then used it while applying a claw hold on Creed. King released the claw and then punched Creed with the glove. King went to the floor and returned with a black bag. King poured thumbtacks from the bag onto the canvas. King and Creed fought, and eventually Creed suplexed King onto the tacks.

Nana stood on the apron with a black bag. Creed pulled a gamer glove out of the bag and put it on his right hand. He eventually threw a punch at King, and pyro shot out of the glove. The live crowd loved it. Bandido and Creed ended up fighting on the stage near a bin. “Fire!” Creed yelled, and then pyro shot out of what Excalibur said was a pyro bin. Page hit Kofi with a light tube and then performed a Deadeye on Creed.

King picked up Swerve on the apron and ended up performing a move that put both men through the table covered in barbed wire. The others returned to ringside. Kofi and Creed put Page on top of four chairs on the floor and then put a window over him. Creed held the window while Kofi jumped from the barricade and Boom Dropped on top of it, causing the glass to break on Page.

The challengers got Bandido in the ring and roughed him up in front of a table covered in barbed wire. Nana held the tab. Kofi and Creed pulled out what McGuinness said were covered padlocks. Kofi hit King with one, and Creed put the other in King’s mouth. Kofi kicked King, who wound up down on the floor covered in blood.

Back in the ring, Page tried to save Bandido from being put through a table. The challengers took out Page. King tried to save his partner as well. Bandido fought free and powerbombed Kofi through the table covered in barbed wire. Nana handed a chain to Swerve, who stood on the middle rope and wrapped the chain around Bandido’s neck. Bandido pulled Swerve off the ropes and removed the chain from his neck.

Bandido performed a top rope frog splash onto Swerve, who was on all fours over the thumbtacks. Swerve fought back and ripped Bandido’s mask, which drew boos from the crowd. Page returned and threw punches at Swerve. Page grabbed a chair, only to have Swerve kick it at his face. Swerve pulled Page over him and opened his mouth to let blood from Page’s forehead go into his mouth. Ugh.

Page worked over Swerve with a chair. Page stood over Swerve with the chair, but Swerve spat Page’s blood in his face. Swerve performed a Deadeye on Page. Swerve went to the stage and came back with a cinder block. Bandido cut off Swerve with a kick before he could hit Page with the cinder block. Bandido wanted to 21 Plex Swerve onto the cinder block, but Creed cut him off. Kofi and Creed performed a double-team move that spiked Bandido’s head on the cinder block.

King pulled Kofi and Creed to the floor and sat them on chairs with a frame wrapped in barbed wire in front of them. King performed a running crossbody block onto the frame. Swerve grabbed King and put him down with a Vertebreaker on the ring steps. Schiavone said that was it for King in this match.

Swerve entered the ring and set up Page for a move, but Page raked his head with barbed wire. Swerve avoided the Deadeye and threw Page over the rope. Page hit him with a Buckshot Lariat from behind. Page followed up with a second. Page went for a third, but Swerve held up a chair that hit Page’s head. The referee started to count both men down. Swerve got to his feet. Page was about to, but Swerve hit him with a House Call Kick, and the referee finished his count.

Swerve Strickland, Kofi, and Austin Creed defeated Hangman Page, Bandido, and Brody King in a Texas Death Match in 39:20 to win the AEW Trios Titles.

Afterward, Swerve laughed maniacally while lying in a corner of the ring and clutching one of the title belts…

Powell’s POV: Shouldn’t the referee have restarted his count once Swerve kicked Page? I didn’t care for the way Swerve won, but I like that he beat Page, because it adds to their ongoing feud. Anyway, the match lived up to the insanity that was expected once they started teasing a Texas Death Match.

Still shots aired from past AEW events in Arizona…

Backstage shots aired of Kyle Fletcher talking with Don Callis, and of Will Ospreay sparring with Mike Bailey while Alex Windsor and Jamie Hayter were in the background…

The broadcast team was shown at their desk. Excalibur said the crew was cleaning up the carnage from the previous match. They recapped Jack Perry using a Muscle Buster on The Beast Mortos to win his pre-show match. Excalibur said Joe was supposed to be at this show, but he was among the people who couldn’t get there due to the airline issues…

A pre-taped Samoa Joe promo aired. Joe said Jack Perry would get his wish because he’s coming to Dynamite and will beat his ass unmercifully in the ring. Joe told him not to worry about shaking his hand because he’s going to do plenty of shaking when Joe strangles him unconscious…

A video package set up the AEW Women’s Tag Team Title match… Harley Cameron and Kris Statlander were featured in a skit. They came up with a bunch of crazy ideas, and then Billy Gunn appeared and asked why they don’t just use pyro. They told him simultaneously to shut the f— up. Tony Khan emerged from a room and said their video package was over and they needed to make their entrance, which they did. Justin Roberts took over the ring announcing duties. The entrnace of the champions followed…

4. “The Brawling Birds” Jamie Hayter and Alex Windsor vs. Harley Cameron and Kris Statlander for the AEW Women’s Tag Team Titles. The crowd was quiet to start following the insanity of the previous match. The fans came to life a bit with some clapping five minutes in. Statlander and Hayter tagged in and then traded blows. Statlander got the better of it and got a near fall before tagging out.

Cameron drilled Statlander with a knee strike. Windsor put Cameron down. The champions suplexed Cameron on top of her partner, and then Hayter covered Cameron for a two count. There was a sequence that resulted in all four wrestlers being down, which drew some applause. They did a chicken spot with Windsor and Cameron trading trikes after ending up on the shoulders of their opponents.

Late in the match, Windsor put Cameron down with a lariat. Hayter followed up with Hayterade and had Cameron pinned, but Statlander broke it up. Statlander dropkicked Hayter and then dragged her partner to their corner where she tagged herself in. Windsor tagged in and the champions ended up going for their finisher, but Cameron grabbed Hayter and pulled her to the floor. Statlander hit Saturday Night Fever on Windsor before pinning her.

Harley Cameron and Kris Statlander beat “The Brawling Birds” Jamie Hayter and Alex Windsor in 12:15 to win the AEW Women’s Tag Team Titles.

Pyro shot off for the title change. The two teams shook hands afterward…

Powell’s POV: I’m not a fan of having the title change take place in this slot. The crowd was respectful, but they never seemed to get fully invested in the match after sitting through Texas Death. Surely, it would have come off better had they saved the title change for Dynamite or Collision.

The ad aired for AEW Dynamite on Thanksgiving Eve and Collision on Thanksgiving night… A video package set up the AEW National Championship match, and then the entrances followed…

5. Tommaso Ciampa vs. Ilja Dragunov for the AEW National Championship. The crowd’s hangover carried over to this match. Ciampa targeted Dragunov’s right arm, which came into play when Dragunov tried to swing in the ropes, only to fall to the mat. Ciampa blasted him with a running knee strike.

Dragunov battled back and targeted Ciampa’s knee. Dragunov’s nose bled as he had a nice run of offense. Ciampa came back and performed an avalanche Air Raid Crash, but he sold his injured leg. Ciampa dumped Dragunov to the floor moments later. Dragunov’s leg appeared to bend a little awkwardly when he landed. Fortunately, he seemed fine.

Ciampa performed an Air Raid Crash on the ring steps. Back in the ring, Ciampa drilled Dragunov with a knee strike and covered him for a near fall. The wrestlers fought on the apron, where Dragunov performed a German suplex, which got a rise out of the crowd. Dragunov dropped off the apron and hit a D-Bomb. Dragunov struggled, but he was able to get Ciampa back in the ring before the referee could count them out.

Dragunov performed a superplex. He went for a D-Bomb, but Ciampa avoided it. Ciampa applied an armbar. Dragunov rolled onto him to break the hold. The wrestlers traded strikes in the middle of the ring, which brought the live crowd to life. Dragunov powerbombed Ciampa and hit him with the D-Bomb before pinning him…

Ilja Dragunov defeated Tommaso Ciampa in 21:00 to win the AEW National Championship.

Powell’s POV: A strong match. The live crowd was quiet for the first half of the match, but they seemed invested and came to life during the closing minutes. Dragunov’s best stretch on the WWE main roster was when he held open challenges as the U.S. Champion. Andrade El Idiolo started the trend of defending the AEW National Championship weekly. Ciampa didn’t hold the belt long enough to show whether he could keep it going, but Dragunov seems like the perfect guy to have weekly title defenses. Well, assuming he’s still in one piece after that nasty Air Raid Crash bump on the edge of the ring steps.

A video package hyped the AEW Continental Classic tournament starting on Wednesday’s Dynamite… Excalibur hyped a selection special for Monday on YouTube…

A video package set up the AEW Women’s Championship match. Marina Shafir made her entrance through the crowd. Shafir wore new blue and black gear (or at least it’s new to me). Mercedes Mone made her entrance…

6. Mercedes Mone vs. Marina Shafir for the AEW Women’s Championship. Justin Roberts delivered in-ring introductions for the title match.
